# Source assessment

[English](source-assessment.md) | [עברית](source-assessment.he.md)

## Rating method

**Source quality**

- **High**: leading peer-reviewed journal, major scholarly work, or authoritative institutional analysis with a clear evidentiary basis.
- **Medium-high**: strong academic or institutional source whose method, publication status or scope creates a material limitation.
- **Medium**: useful supporting or interpretive source that should not carry the central claim alone.

**Claim fit**

- **High**: directly supports the mechanism or claim for which it is cited.
- **Medium**: supports the mechanism, but requires an explicit transfer from another market, period or institutional setting.
- **Contextual**: informs interpretation but does not establish the claim by itself.

The ratings assess the use of each source in this project. They are not general rankings of the authors or their bodies of work.

## Limits of the evidence

The evidence is strong enough to justify scenario planning, but not to predict an AI bubble or prescribe one universal policy instrument.

Three transfer problems remain important:

1. Much of the crisis-resilience evidence concerns regulated banks and housing credit, while AI investment also uses corporate bonds, private credit, special-purpose vehicles, leases and long-term capacity commitments.
2. Historical technological waves differ in capital intensity, speed of diffusion, market concentration and dependence on energy and compute.
3. Evidence that resources can be reallocated after a downturn does not guarantee that they will remain in the same country or continue to support AI innovation.

These limitations are part of the conclusion. They explain why the first policy step is exposure mapping rather than a prediction or a ready-made intervention.
